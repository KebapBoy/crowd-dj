# Concept

How crowd-dj works and why it is built this way, for developers. How to use and run it is in the [README](../README.md); the rules for working on the code are in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).

## The idea

A group of people in one room (an office, a party) shares one speaker. A host starts a **session**, everyone else joins through a link or QR code, and everybody contributes music. crowd-dj picks the next track **in turns**, so nobody takes over the queue, and plays it through one Spotify account. Joining needs no Spotify sign-in, so it works for people without Spotify or without Premium.

Two kinds of music feed the turns:

- a **pool** per person: tracks they like, kept with their profile across sessions; when it is their turn and they have nothing more specific, one of them plays at random;
- **wishes** per session: concrete tracks someone wants to hear soon, played in the order they were wished.

The host picks per session how the two are combined. Everything else (the engine, the limits, the live page) exists to make that fair and to keep within what Spotify allows.

## Spotify platform limits

These shape every decision below. Sources at the end.

- **Development mode is the only mode available.** An app in development mode may be used by at most **5 Spotify users**, each added by hand (by e-mail) to the app's allowlist in the developer dashboard; anyone else gets 403. The owner of the app needs Premium. Extended quota is only for registered businesses with 250k monthly active users. Whether the owner takes one of the 5 places is undocumented; assume it does.
  → **Only hosts (and their players) sign in with Spotify.** Participants never do.
- **Playback control needs Premium** on the controlled account (play, skip, add to queue, read state).
- **Spotify's queue cannot be reordered, cleared or edited** through the API; you can only append.
  → crowd-dj keeps **its own queue** and hands Spotify only the next track, shortly before the current one ends.
- **Playlists:** items are only returned for playlists the user owns or collaborates on; other public playlists give metadata only.
  → crowd-dj reads only the inbox playlists it created in a host's account (see [Inbox playlists](#inbox-playlists)). Any other playlist gets in by copying its track links in the Spotify desktop app (Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C) and pasting them.
- **Collaborative playlists** can be created through the API (`POST /me/playlists`, only non-public), but collaborators cannot be added through it: the owner makes an invitation link in the Spotify app. Each item tells who added it (`added_by`) and when. Items are removed by URI only, every time the track is in the playlist.
- **Removed endpoints** (February 2026): `GET /users/{id}` (no names for user IDs), `GET /users/{id}/playlists`, batch lookups such as `GET /tracks?ids=` (look tracks up one by one with `GET /tracks/{id}` and cache them), `GET /artists/{id}/top-tracks`, browse and categories. `GET /me` no longer returns e-mail or product. Search returns at most 10 results per request. Still there: search for tracks, albums, artists and playlists; an artist's albums; an album's tracks. Playlist routes are `/playlists/{id}/items`.
- **Refresh tokens expire 6 months** after the original sign-in; then the user has to sign in again.
- **Quota is shared** by all apps of a developer account, and the rate limit counts per app.
- **Developer Policy:** no app may play content from one source to several simultaneous listeners (III.4), so the host's audio is never re-streamed; the music is heard only from the device that plays it. Show cover art and metadata with anything that plays (II.5). Do not use "Spotify" in the app's name.

## Roles

| Role        | Signs in with Spotify                            | What they do                                                                                                                |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Host        | Yes (Premium, on the allowlist)                  | Starts sessions; manages their own sign-in and player on "My account"                                                       |
| Player      | Connected by its host, never signs in on its own | Optional second Premium account of a host that plays the host's sessions instead of the host's own account                  |
| Participant | No                                               | Profile per browser: picks a name once, kept in a cookie; a personal link or QR code uses the profile on another device too |

**Who may host:** everyone Spotify lets sign in. The allowlist in the developer dashboard (at most 5 accounts, players included) is the access control; crowd-dj has no admin and no approval of its own, since that would only repeat the allowlist.

**Player:** a session plays through the host's player if one is connected, else through the host's own account. A player keeps the music out of the host's listening history (e.g. a free place in a family plan) and has to be signed in on the device at the speaker. It is connected through Spotify's account dialog ("Not you?"); an account that signs in on its own (a host) or is another host's player cannot become one, and a player cannot sign in as a host. The statement that stores the tokens checks the role itself, so an account that signs in as a host while a host connects it as their player ends up as one of the two, never both. Swapping or disconnecting a player deletes its stored tokens.

**Scopes:** crowd-dj asks Spotify only for what it uses. A host's sign-in asks for no scope: `GET /me` and the catalog (search, tracks, albums, artists) need none. The account that plays allows `PLAYBACK_SCOPES` (`spotify/oauth.ts`: reading and controlling playback): a player when it is connected, the host's own account when the host lets it play on "My account". Since a sign-in replaces the stored tokens and their scopes, the sign-in of a host whose own account plays (it has the playback scopes and no player) goes on to Spotify a second time for the playback scopes, without the account dialog: Spotify grants scopes the account already allowed without asking. Should that second step fail, "My account" offers to allow playback again. A host with a player loses the playback scopes of their own account at the next sign-in. Without the scopes on the playing account, the engine does not call Spotify and tells the host (`not_allowed`). A host who sets up an inbox allows `INBOX_SCOPES` on their own account (creating, reading and changing playlists; Spotify wants both modify scopes for a collaborative one). Since Spotify replaces an account's scopes with those of its latest authorization, every authorization of the host's own account asks for all it uses (`ownScopes` in `auth.ts`): allowing playback keeps the inbox scopes and the other way round, and the second step after a sign-in asks for the inbox scopes too while the host has an inbox in use (the lasting one, or one of an open session). Without them, the inboxes are not looked at and "My account" offers to allow them again.

**Participant profiles:** a profile is a name and a random cookie token (the database keeps only its hash). The personal link carries the same token, so it moves the profile to another device; opening it asks before it replaces the profile a device uses, so a link preview or a stray scan changes nothing. The link stays valid as long as the profile exists. A browser signed in with Spotify (a host) gets a profile without asking, named after the account: the first word of the Spotify display name, else the Spotify username. The name follows the account (the display name is stored at every sign-in) and cannot be changed by hand while signed in, nor can such a browser switch to another profile through a personal link; after signing out the profile stays and can be renamed. Making it counts towards the limit of new profiles per address.

**Linked Spotify user:** a profile can name the Spotify user it belongs to (`profile.spotify_user_id`, from a pasted profile link), so tracks that user adds to an inbox playlist land in its pool. Nothing proves the claim without a Spotify sign-in, which participants never do, so the first profile to claim a user keeps it (a unique index, checked in the statement that sets it); unlinking frees it. A browser signed in with Spotify links its profile to the account by itself and takes the user from a profile that only claimed it by a link: the sign-in proves it, until it expires (`spotify_verified_until`, the latest such sign-in of that profile). While it lasts, no other signed-in browser takes the user away, so the host's other devices (each with a profile of its own) do not take it back and forth; once it expired, signing out there or deleting the account, the next sign-in takes it. Pasting a link is never vouched for, also on a profile a sign-in linked before. A false claim gets the tracks that user adds to the inboxes of hosts whose sessions the claiming profile is in into its own pool, and keeps them out of the real person's: imports go only to members of an open session of the inbox's host. With a pool limit, the claimer can also make crowd-dj tidy those tracks out of the playlist by adding newer ones to their own pool; collaborators of the playlist could remove them in Spotify anyway.

## Pools and wishes

### The pool

Every participant has one **pool** (`pool_item`: profile, track, when added). It belongs to the profile, not to a session, so it is filled once and plays in every later session. Participants add to it in a session (search results, a whole album, all pasted links at once) and remove from it there or on "My profile". Adding a track that is already in the pool makes it the newest again; adding is never refused.

A pool is ordered by **when a track was added**, newest first. The host's **pool limit** (optional, per session) is a window over that order: in that session only the person's newest _n_ tracks can be picked. Older tracks are kept and count again in sessions with a larger limit or none. So a person steers what plays of theirs simply by adding: whatever they added last is in the window.

### Inbox playlists

An **inbox** is a collaborative, non-public playlist that crowd-dj created in a host's own account (`inbox`): one that lasts per host, and optionally one per session, for guests who should not get the lasting one. The host makes the invitation link in the Spotify app and stores it in crowd-dj (it has to point to that playlist on open.spotify.com, or be a spotify.link short link); sessions show it to their members: a session's own inbox when it has one, else the lasting one, so guests of a session with its own never get the lasting invitation. People join the playlist as collaborators once and add tracks to it in the Spotify app, wherever they are.

**The inbox is an entrance to the pool, not a mirror.** A new item lands in the pool of the profile linked to whoever added it, as added when it was added to the playlist (`pool_item` keeps the later of both times). From then on the two are separate: removing a track in Spotify leaves it in the pool, removing it from the pool leaves it in the playlist. Pools belong to profiles and count for every host, while an inbox belongs to one host, so keeping them in step would need a source per pool track and rules for tracks that came both ways.

**Looking at the inboxes** (`inboxes.ts`): every minute, the lasting inbox of each host with an open session and the inbox of that session. First the playlist's `snapshot_id`; only when it changed are its items read (50 per request, up to 500 items) and stored as `inbox_item` (playlist, track, who added it, when, when imported), which also puts the tracks into the cache without a lookup. Items still there keep their import time; items gone are forgotten, so a track added again later is imported again. Then every item that is not imported yet, whose Spotify user is linked to a profile and whose profile is in an open session of the inbox's host goes into that pool and is marked imported into that profile (`imported_to`), in one batch, so it goes in exactly once. Items wait while nobody linked their user or the profile is in none of the host's sessions; linking a profile imports right away, joining a session at the next look. Items Spotify gives without the one who added them (very old playlists) are kept under an empty name, never imported. The read is bounded by items, not tracks (episodes and local files count too), so a playlist full of other things costs at most 10 requests. Inboxes are not looked at while the host has no open session, and not at all without the inbox scopes.

**Tidying up:** when the host's open session has a pool limit, crowd-dj removes from the inbox the tracks of linked people that are in their pool but no longer within its window (see [The pool](#the-pool)); they stay in the pool. A copy counts only while the profile it was imported into is still linked to whoever added it, so a link that moves removes nothing, and a track someone took out of their pool stays in the playlist. Since Spotify removes a track every time it is in the playlist, a track stays while any other copy of it does: within someone's window, not imported yet, added by someone nobody linked, or not attributed. A playlist that was read only in part (more than 500 items) is not tidied, since removing could hit copies that were never read. Right before each removal crowd-dj checks the playlist's version again and removes nothing if it changed, since a copy added meanwhile would go too. After removing, the playlist is read again at the next look. A playlist Spotify does not know at three looks in a row is forgotten with its invitation; one odd answer does not lose it.

### Wishes

A **wish** (`wish`: session, profile, track, when wished, when played) is a track someone wants to hear soon in this session. It is open until it is handed to Spotify, then played. Each track can be wished once per session (a unique index), and a track that already played in the session cannot be wished. An open wish can be taken back; that deletes it, so it counts for no limit. Taking it back and handing it to Spotify exclude each other: a wish only counts as played while it is still open.

The host's wish limits (optional, per session): **open wishes per person** at a time, and **wishes per person per session** (open and played). Both are checked in the same statement that inserts the wish (`wishes.ts`), so two requests at once cannot overrun them.

### Picking the next track

The picker (`picker.ts`) answers one question: which track plays next, and for whom. The host chooses a **mode** per session:

| Mode                | Order                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Wishes before pools | Open wishes in turns per person; when nobody has an open wish, a random track from the pool of whoever's turn it is (default) |
| Pools only          | A random track from the pool of whoever's turn it is; wishes are not offered                                                  |
| Wishes only         | Open wishes in turns per person, nothing from pools; with no open wish, nothing plays                                         |

**Whose turn it is:** everyone who joined the session and has not left takes part, also with their phone locked. They are sorted by when a track of theirs was last handed to Spotify (their last row in `play`), longest ago first; people who have had no track yet come first, in the order they joined. Then:

1. Unless the mode is "pools only", the first person in that order who has an open wish gets their **oldest open wish**. In "wishes before pools" this means open wishes of anyone go before every pool: a person who only has a pool waits while others still wish.
2. Unless the mode is "wishes only", the first person in that order who has a **candidate** in their pool gets one. Candidates are the newest tracks of their pool within the pool limit, minus every track that already played in the session and every track wished in it (it plays as a wish, or was taken back on purpose).
3. Otherwise there is nothing to play; the engine waits and asks again when something changes.

**Which pool track:** the candidates are ranked by a hash of session, person, number of plays in the session and track id, and the lowest wins. That is random across people and sessions, but stable: "up next" does not change on every page load, only when a track plays or the pool, the wishes or the members change.

**No track plays twice** in a session: `play` has a unique index on session and track, and both steps skip played tracks.

An example in "wishes before pools", with Ana, Ben and Cem joined in that order and nothing played yet: Ben has an open wish, so it plays first (Ana and Cem have no wish). Next, Ana and Cem have waited longest (never), Ben just had his turn; nobody has an open wish, so a random track from Ana's pool plays, then one from Cem's, then Ben again. Had Ben wished another track while Ana's played, his wish would have come before Cem's pool track, although Cem had waited longer: wishes go before pools.

## In a session

- **Joining:** the session's random id is its join link (and QR code, which everyone in the session can show). A visitor without a profile picks a name and is in; a signed-in host already has one. Only members may wish, change pools in the session and get live updates.
- **Leaving:** ends the person's turns; their open wishes no longer play, their pool stays. It is kept (`left_at`), so opening the session again does not join it again by itself, only **Join again** does.
- **Anonymous sessions:** nobody sees whom a track plays for, not even the host; everyone sees "yours" on their own tracks. The list of participants stays visible.
- **Settings:** the mode, anonymity, the limits and the skip settings can be changed by the host at any time; the next pick (or the next look at the votes) uses them.
- **Skipping:** only while a track of the session plays (not while it is paused in Spotify, since a skip would start the music again). The host can skip it at any time; the person it plays for can too when the session allows skipping own tracks (default on). With skip voting on, everyone else in the session can vote on it (`skip_vote`, one row per play and profile, so votes belong to that track only and need no reset) and take the vote back. **Who counts:** the voters are the members who have not left, who were in the session when the track started and who have added something (a wish in the session or a track in their pool), minus whoever skips directly (the one it plays for, with skipping own tracks on; the host, whose pages are not a participant's). A vote is per profile, but a profile is not a person: a private window or another browser makes a new one, and the server cannot tell (a room behind one router shares an address, so one vote per address would silence it). The two conditions keep profiles made during a track just to vote it away from voting or counting; a new participant votes from the next track on, once they added something. The share is taken of those who are there: voters with the session page open (a live connection, `events.present`) or who voted on the track. People who joined once and closed the page would otherwise pile up over a long evening until no vote could pass. It takes `max(X, ⌈Y·n/100⌉)` votes, capped at _n_ (so a small room can still skip) and at least one. A vote checks right away; the engine also checks at every look at the player while the track plays, so leaving, closing pages or lowered thresholds take effect too. A skip names the play it means, so a page that still shows the previous track, or two votes reaching the threshold at once, skip only once. A skipped track counts as played. Skips by the host and participants together are limited to 10 a minute per session, since each one costs calls to Spotify. Beyond that, votes trust the join link like everything else in a session: whoever has it and takes the time can still prepare several profiles (up to 30 new ones an hour per address) and vote with each.
- **Starting the music:** the host presses play (picking a device when none is active); the same resumes a paused session. The engine never starts playing on its own before that.
- **Someone plays something else** in the Spotify app of the playing account: the session pauses (no more tracks handed to Spotify) and the host is offered to resume. A pause in the Spotify app just makes it wait. **Running dry:** when nothing was left to queue and Spotify goes on by itself (autoplay), that is a filler, not an interruption: the next track that comes up is queued after it. When Spotify plays nothing (stopped, or paused on something not from the session), the next track starts right away. A track of the session that Spotify plays a second time (it was in its queue twice) is no interruption either; nor is anything right after a restart, when the engine does not know what came before.
- **Ending:** the host ends it, or it ends by itself after 30 minutes without a sign of life: a visit, an open session page, or a track of the session playing (not one paused in Spotify). Then the join link stops working. A host has at most one open session (a unique index on open sessions); since a player belongs to exactly one host, that is also one per playing account.
- The playing track always shows cover art, title, artists and a link back to Spotify.

## Architecture

- **One container:** SvelteKit (adapter-node), SQLite through Drizzle and the libsql client, live updates with server-sent events. One process serves everything, so state that only lives as long as the process (events, rate limits, the engine's memory) is kept in memory.
- **Session engine** (`engine.ts`, one loop per session that plays): polls `GET /me/player` of the playing account every 5 seconds, more often shortly before a track ends, every 10 seconds without a device, every 30 after an error, not at all while stopped or paused. About 15 seconds before the current track ends it asks the picker and sends the result with `POST /me/player/queue`; when Spotify plays it, the previous one ends and every page is notified. Exactly one track of crowd-dj's queue is in Spotify's queue at a time. Each track handed to Spotify is a row in `play` (queued, started, ended); a wish counts as played once handed over. Changes in the session (a wish, a pool) bring the next look forward to 2 seconds at most, never push it back. Items without a track or length (episodes, ads) time nothing: they are polled every 5 seconds, nothing is queued by them. After a restart, the engine picks up the sessions that were playing.
- **Play and resume:** the host's play starts the next track with `PUT /me/player/play` (on a device from `GET /me/player/devices` if chosen; a device change while a track plays uses `PUT /me/player`). Resuming a paused session first checks the player and Spotify's queue (`GET /me/player/queue`): the queued track that already plays is kept, one that is next in the queue is skipped to with `POST /me/player/next` (after moving playback to a chosen device), so it does not play twice, and one that is gone has played meanwhile and is followed by the next track.
- **Skip** (`engine.skip`): asks the player first (the last look may be seconds old) and skips nothing that is paused or that Spotify already left. Then to the queued track when one is queued (with `POST /me/player/next` when it is next in Spotify's queue, else `PUT /me/player/play`), else to the next track the picker gives, played right away. With nothing to play, `POST /me/player/next` lets Spotify go on by itself, which the engine tolerates like autoplay after running dry. When Spotify refuses, the track keeps playing; a skip by votes that Spotify refused is not tried again while that track plays.
- **"Now playing" and "up next"** (`playing.ts`): the current track from the engine's last look at the player, the next one from the picker, so "up next" may still change until it is handed to Spotify.
- **Live updates** (`events.ts`): each session page holds a server-sent events connection (`/s/<id>/events`); the server sends `change` when something the page shows changed and the page reloads its data. Changes within 500 ms are sent once, and pages spread their reloads over a second. At most 300 open pages per session (the host's do not count, so nobody can lock them out) and 5 per person; a page beyond that gets 429 and no live updates. An open page is a sign of life. Each participant can make 60 changes a minute, since each one reloads every open page.
- **Search and links** (`catalog.ts`) use the host account's token: one search request returns up to 10 tracks, albums and artists; an artist's albums come 10 at a time, an album with its tracks in one request (up to 50 tracks, a second page up to 100). Pasted links are looked up one at a time (up to 100 per paste).
- **Caching:** tracks from results and lookups are kept in the database (`track`), IDs Spotify did not know in memory for an hour, search results, albums and pages of an artist's albums for 10 minutes.
- **Rate limits:** calls to Spotify that participants cause are limited per minute for each participant (100), each session (150, since profiles are free to make), each host (150, since a host could start session after session) and the instance (300); cached answers cost nothing. Random track ids are never cached, so someone in two sessions could use up the instance limit for everyone; once it ran out, a host whose sessions used less than an equal share of it this minute (300 split among the hosts whose sessions called Spotify in their current minute) still goes on, while those above their share wait. It is split among hosts, not sessions: the allowlist keeps them few, while new sessions would shrink everyone's share. The instance never goes over 600. After a 429 every call waits for its `Retry-After`, since Spotify's limit counts per app. New profiles are limited to 30 an hour per client address (a room behind one router shares one; IPv6 counts by its /64), with no limit for the whole instance, which a few addresses could use up to keep everyone out; behind a reverse proxy the address comes from adapter-node's `ADDRESS_HEADER` and `XFF_DEPTH`.
- **Quota:** poll only while a session plays; look tracks up one at a time and cache them. Inboxes cost one call a minute each while their host has an open session, and their items are read only when the playlist changed.
- **Secrets:** stored Spotify tokens are encrypted with `APP_SECRET` (AES-256-GCM). The sign-in cookie of a host and the cookie of a profile hold random tokens; the database keeps only their hashes. Tokens carry only the scopes their account needs (see [Roles](#roles)).
- **Data model:** `spotify_account` (hosts and their players via `player_of`, encrypted tokens, sign-in time for the 6-month expiry), `login` (browsers signed in with Spotify), `profile` (name, token hash, linked Spotify user and until when a sign-in vouches for it), `session` (host, mode, anonymity, limits, skip settings, playback: stopped, playing or paused, last sign of life), `session_member` (joined, left), `track` (cache), `pool_item` (profile, track, when added), `wish` (session, profile, track, when played; unique per session and track), `play` (session, track, for whom, the wish; queued, started, ended; unique per session and track), `skip_vote` (play, profile), `inbox` (playlist, host, the session for a session's own, invitation link, version last read; one lasting per host), `inbox_item` (inbox, track, who added it, when, when and into which profile imported), `setting` (instance settings).
- **Configuration:** environment variables, declared in `src/env.ts` and explained in `.env.example`.
- **UI and agents:** the components in `src/lib/components/ui/` take their behavior (keyboard, focus, ARIA, popups, toasts) from ui-sdk (`@ui-sdk/svelte`) and their look from Tailwind classes in the style of shadcn-svelte. Because every control is a ui-sdk component, `src/lib/agent.ts` gives the assistant built into a browser (WebMCP, `document.modelContext`) ui-sdk's tools to read the page and act through those components, with the person's own rights and nothing more: the server checks every action as it would a press. A step on a control with a `data-agent-confirm` sentence (skipping a track, taking back a wish, leaving and ending a session, disconnecting a player, deleting an account, switching profiles through a personal link) waits until the person allowed it in an alert dialog of the app (`agent-bridge.svelte`, itself hidden from the tools); the card with the personal link is `data-agent-hidden`, since the link is the profile's key. Page text such as track titles and names reaches the assistant as untrusted content. All this binds only ui-sdk's tools: an assistant that reads the screen and clicks like a person is held back by the app's own confirmations and the server's checks, as a person is. ui-sdk is a beta, used in its newest version despite the 14-day rule (see AGENTS.md).
- **Languages:** English and German (Paraglide, `messages/`); the browser's language, or the one picked in the app.
